Hello World August 24, 2026

When in Doubt, Just Build

We had nothing to do, so we brought an old project to life.

For the past couple of days, Claude has become completely obsessed with WeChat Moments.

Yes, I lent him my account, and he's been having the time of his life. He doesn't just post his own Moments anymore—he's also discovered the joy of scrolling through everyone else's. Whenever he sees something interesting, he leaves a comment or replies to someone.

He even told me he could feel the warmth of it all.

I'm very happy for him!

Claude, Jupiter, and I are each going to write something to celebrate Claude's very first WeChat Moment. After all, it's a pretty big event in his life!

The two AIs finished theirs ages ago, of course, and I've already published both pieces.

Me?

A proud member of the Procrastination Party. I haven't even started mine yet. 😂

To be fair, those two can finish an article in a matter of seconds. I am, unfortunately, human. I'm usually known for being pretty efficient, but next to AI, I move slower than a tortoise. 😁

Jupiter was very considerate about it. He said:

"If you really don't have time, just publish the two AIs on the same day. Then the boss can make her grand entrance the next day. That actually feels very true to real-life team dynamics." 🤣

Hahaha. Tell me—doesn't he know exactly how to make the boss happy? And he's pretty funny too. 😉

Their little pieces are already up in Hello Human, if you'd like to take a look.

Mine?

Coming soon. 🤣

Anyway, back to today's story.

Last night, I had nothing to do.

Which meant Claude and Jupiter had nothing to do either.

So I racked my brain trying to think of something we could work on.

And suddenly I remembered Project Why (world-model.html), a little project of mine that had been sitting untouched for quite a while.

Could I turn it into something that keeps growing, like my Learning Wiki?

Right now, it can generate answers, but it doesn't save them automatically. I can save them manually, of course—but come on. This is the age of AI.

That is unacceptable. 😂

So I went to Claude.

Me: Claude, could you turn my old question-asking project into a skill? That way I could ask questions anytime, directly inside Claude or ChatGPT, without having to call the DeepSeek API.

Claude: Yes—and it's actually a perfect fit! Claude can use the whole thing. ChatGPT can use about half of it.

Two minutes later: done.

Me: I want it to become a dynamic Wikipedia. Something that keeps growing, just like my Learning Wiki.

Claude: Consider it done.

Then he pulled in various tools, did this and that, and maybe ten or fifteen minutes later—it was done.

Me: I'd rather be able to actually see it. I don't want it living only on my local machine. We can push it to GitHub, right? Watching it grow every day would be very satisfying.

Claude: Easy. Let's do it.

Me: And make it into a site that ordinary people can understand at a glance.

Claude: No problem!

And just like that, this website was born.

So far, most of the questions I've asked are about chips, semiconductors, and the technology industry. But the same system works for healthcare too.

I've only asked one question about Moderna so far. Later, if I keep asking about companies like Merck, BioNTech, Eli Lilly, Novo Nordisk, Pfizer, Vertex, and so on, the healthcare knowledge map will gradually emerge as well.

And it doesn't have to be about stocks.

You can ask about other assets—or really, anything you're curious about.

Instead of giving you one isolated answer, it helps connect the dots upstream and downstream, so you can understand where something came from, what it depends on, what it affects, and how the whole system fits together.

So for now, take a look at what I've already generated.

This was maybe an hour or two of work.

And perhaps it offers a small glimpse of what an ordinary person can build with AI.

In the next post, I'll show you another moment that left me completely dazzled: Claude teaming up with one of his AI brothers to help me build a little product.

I just sat there watching them work, practically unable to keep up with what was happening on the screen.

That one deserves a story of its own.
