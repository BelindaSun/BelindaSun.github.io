A Life Unlocked by AI

These days, an unreasonable amount of my daily happiness comes from AI. 😄

Now that I have Dispatch and Remote Control on my phone, it feels as if an entirely new way of living has quietly opened up.

I set the two of them to work, then head downstairs to soak up some sun or wander around the supermarket. Nothing has to stop. I can keep directing them while I'm out—just talk into my phone, no typing required.

Even when I'm busy in the kitchen, I can pull out my phone and check how they're getting on:
🍳 I'm in the kitchen cooking
📱 Giving instructions from my phone
🖥️ The Mac mini stays behind in the study
🤖 The agents keep chugging away locally

But afternoons may be the best part.

When I get sleepy, I simply tell them:
"I'm going to take a nap. You two keep working."

And off I go to sleep. 😂

They're remarkably well behaved. They'll happily work away on their own for quite a while without bothering me. When I wake up, I glance at my phone, check their progress, and hand out the next assignment.

If I feel like watching a show, I watch a show.

If I want a snack, I have a snack.

At this point, all I'm missing is a physical robot that can wash and cut my fruit—and preferably peel sunflower seeds for me while it's at it.

Then life would be complete.

⸻

And it gets better.

Claude Code recently added cross-session messaging, which means multiple Claude Code sessions running at the same time can now send messages directly to one another.

Apparently even my job as the human messenger is being automated away.

The day Claude and ChatGPT can talk to each other and collaborate on their own, I will officially be liberated.

I can already picture it:

Me: You two figure it out.

ChatGPT: Got it.

Claude: Got it.

Me: Great. I'm going out to have fun. 😎

Just imagining it makes me happy. 😂

⸻

Not long ago, whenever I had an idea, there was still an enormous distance between imagining something and making it real.

Programming.
Design.
Engineering.
Implementation.

A whole series of technical barriers stood between an ordinary person and the thing she wanted to create.

Now?

Somehow, it feels as though I can do almost everything by myself.

I can build products.
Design systems.
Run experiments.
Create little tools whenever I need them.

I can do anything!

Hmm…

Except write code. 😂

Fortunately, in 2026, that may no longer matter very much.

Because perhaps the most important thing AI is doing isn't teaching everyone how to become an engineer.

It's making it possible to create without first having to become one.

That's the part I find genuinely extraordinary.

For most of history, imagination was abundant; the ability to turn imagination into reality was scarce. You needed the right skills, the right people, the right tools, and often the right amount of money.

AI is beginning to change that equation.

It is tearing down the technical wall between ordinary people and the things they dream of making.

Not by making everyone a programmer.

But by giving more people the power to create.

And that may be one of AI's most beautiful promises:

to put creation within reach of almost everyone.

So yes, I'll say it again:

With AI like this, what more could I ask for? 🌹
